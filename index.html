A sunlit room. On the wall: Stay with the light for a moment. In the dust of the sunbeam: in a moment, you will blink. The lids close: the world goes out.

It is not dark behind your eyelids. It is red. Further in, even the red goes out: eigengrau, German for intrinsic grey, the colour the eye sees in perfect darkness. But something stays: the last thing you saw, burned in.

Behind the eyes, soft lights appear. Here, the light is yours. Someone’s face. A room you have never been in. Something you almost remember. The light builds until it is everything, then goes out.

The mind rebuilds the room from memory. Not the room. A copy. The window was not here. It had four legs. Most of it, you will not keep.

About four hundred million blinks in a life: two years with your eyes closed.

The eyes open. On the wall: It waited for you. Evening comes. The room fades into one point of light. Everything you have ever seen, you saw in the dark.
